Kitchen renovations in heritage homes in Hobart

In short: If your home is on the Tasmanian Heritage Register, kitchen works need Tasmanian Heritage Council approval. That is often a minor works approval when no significant fabric is affected. If it is locally heritage listed or in a heritage precinct, City of Hobart says you will usually need planning approval, so check with Council before work starts.

Hobart has many older homes, and a lot of them are heritage listed. This guide explains the two heritage lists and how they affect a kitchen renovation, with links to official sources. It is not legal or planning advice.

Last updated: 26 November 2026

This page summarises official information so you know what to expect. It is not legal or planning advice. Heritage Tasmania, City of Hobart or your renovator can confirm what applies to your home.

Two different heritage lists

A Hobart property can be heritage listed in two separate ways, and each has its own approval process:

  • State listing: the Tasmanian Heritage Register, under the Historic Cultural Heritage Act 1995. City of Hobart says proposals for work to these places are referred to the Tasmanian Heritage Council.
  • Local listing: local heritage places and heritage precincts in the Hobart Local Provisions Schedule of the Tasmanian Planning Scheme. That scheme replaced the Hobart Interim Planning Scheme 2015.

Sources: City of Hobart, Heritage places and precincts; City of Hobart, Tasmanian Planning Scheme and Hobart LPS.

How to check your home

  • Tasmanian Heritage Register: search the Discover Heritage portal or the heritage layer on LISTMap, or search the Register entries list by street name. Heritage Tasmania can also issue a Certificate for Affected Place, for a fee, as an official record. That is often used in conveyancing. See Heritage Tasmania, Search the register.
  • Local listing: City of Hobart's planning scheme page has an address search and links to the Tasmanian Planning Scheme viewer and Table C6.1, Local Heritage Places. Council officers can also tell you whether a property is on the state register.

Homes on the Tasmanian Heritage Register

Under the Historic Cultural Heritage Act 1995, you must not carry out works to a registered place unless the Heritage Council approves them. Approval is either a minor works approval or a discretionary permit. Before 1 January 2025, a minor works approval was called a Certificate of Exemption.

The Heritage Council's Works Guidelines (Version 2, October 2025) list kitchen-related work that can be eligible for a minor works approval, including:

  • upgrading a kitchen, bathroom or laundry fit-out with no changes to historic door or window openings, and no impact on significant joinery, walls, ceilings or floors, or on significant archaeological deposits below the floor
  • removing and replacing deteriorated fixtures and fittings, such as kitchen cabinets and taps, that are not historic fabric, where significant walls, floors and skirtings won't be damaged
  • replacing defective wiring or plumbing, as long as fittings that contribute to the heritage character, such as historic switches, outlets or tapware, aren't removed.

A discretionary permit application is needed for work such as removing historic fittings that clearly contribute to the place's heritage value (the guidelines' example is a kitchen's original iron stove), or alterations that disturb or remove significant walls, floors, ceilings, fireplaces, stairs, joinery or decorative plasterwork.

Even if you are confident the work is minor, the Heritage Council says you still need a minor works approval confirming it. Within the City of Hobart, minor works approval applications can be lodged online through PlanBuild Tasmania. A minor works approval doesn't remove any need for a council development application. Without one, you lodge a development application with Council, which forwards it to the Heritage Council. Heritage Tasmania encourages talking to one of its heritage advisors before lodging, and says the advice is free.

Sources: Heritage Tasmania, Getting approval; Heritage Tasmania, Works Guidelines.

Locally listed homes and heritage precincts

City of Hobart says that if your property is heritage listed or in a heritage precinct under the Tasmanian Planning Scheme, you will usually need planning approval for any use or development of the site. Its heritage FAQs say some work is exempt, but that you need advice specific to your property, so check with Council before starting. Council stresses that heritage listing doesn't mean a home can't be changed. It means changes are made carefully, "doing as much as necessary but as little as possible".

In heritage precincts, buildings are classed as contributory, non-contributory or neutral. For contributory buildings, Council says it is important to keep what is significant, which can include original features and materials. Council also runs a Heritage Grants program for owners of heritage-listed properties. It says the program is advertised about every two years.

Source: City of Hobart, Hobart LPS heritage FAQs.

Planning a kitchen around heritage fabric

The Works Guidelines list the kinds of internal fabric worth keeping in a heritage home: the overall room layout, fireplaces and chimney breasts, original floor, wall and ceiling linings, skirtings, architraves, cornices and ceiling roses, and fixtures such as mantels, panelling, doors, window sashes and built-in shelving. In practice, that means:

  • designing new cabinetry to sit within the existing room and openings, rather than moving walls or windows
  • scribing new joinery around mouldings instead of cutting them back (the guidelines list built-in fixtures scribed around skirtings and cornices as eligible for minor works approval)
  • keeping any historic fittings that are removed at the property, with small items bagged and labelled, as the guidelines advise.

Heritage approvals sit on top of the usual building and plumbing rules. Our kitchen renovation process guide covers Tasmania's building and plumbing categories, licences and contracts.

Questions to ask a kitchen renovator

  • Have you worked on heritage-listed homes in Hobart before?
  • Can you supply the photos, plans and product details needed for a minor works approval or planning application?
  • Who will lodge the heritage or planning application, and is that time in the schedule and quote?
  • Which existing features will stay, and how will they be protected during the work?
